The basics

What is an AIF?

An Alternative Investment Fund (AIF) is a privately pooled, SEBI-registered vehicle that invests in assets and strategies outside conventional stocks and bonds — private equity, venture capital, private credit, real assets and long-short, among others. The minimum investment is ₹1 crore per investor.

SEBI groups AIFs into three categories. Category I backs areas the regulator sees as economically useful — venture capital, SME, infrastructure and social-impact funds. Category II covers most private equity and private credit funds — no significant leverage, close-ended, typically a 3-year-plus tenure. Category III runs more active or complex strategies and may use leverage and derivatives — long-short and other hedge-fund-style approaches sit here.

Category I and II funds usually draw down your committed capital in tranches as deals are made, and return it as investments are exited, over several years. Category III funds are generally invested upfront and are more liquid. In all cases these are long-horizon, illiquid commitments — read the private placement memorandum carefully.

Why it matters

Why invest in AIFs

For investors who already hold listed equity and debt, AIFs are how you add exposures the public markets can't give you:

  • Access to private markets — private equity, venture and private credit — outside MF and PMS
  • Return streams that need not move in step with listed equities
  • Specialist managers, and deal flow that isn't otherwise available to individuals
  • A genuine diversifier for a portfolio that is already well covered in public markets
  • The potential for differentiated returns in exchange for locking capital up longer

The cost of that is illiquidity, a multi-year commitment, wide dispersion between managers, and fee structures that usually include a performance share — so manager selection matters more here than anywhere else.
