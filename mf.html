The basics

What is a mutual fund?

A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it, through a SEBI-registered Asset Management Company, in a diversified basket of securities managed to a stated objective. Your holding is measured in units, priced each business day at the fund's net asset value (NAV).

You can invest a lump sum or a fixed amount every month through a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP), and redeem on any business day (subject to any exit load or lock-in). Funds are grouped by what they hold and how — equity, debt, hybrid, index and international, among others — so there is usually a category suited to a given goal and time horizon.

Every scheme comes in two forms. A regular plan pays an ongoing commission to a distributor out of the fund; a direct plan does not, so its expense ratio is lower and more of the return compounds for you. We keep you on direct plans wherever it makes sense.

Why it matters

Why invest in mutual funds

Mutual funds are the simplest way to hold a diversified, professionally managed portfolio, and for most investors they are the core of one:

  • Instant diversification, even with a small amount invested
  • Start a SIP from as little as ₹500 a month and let it compound
  • Redeem on any business day — among the most liquid ways to invest
  • Direct plans keep the distributor commission out of your returns
  • A regulated structure with daily disclosure of NAV and periodic disclosure of holdings
  • A category and fund for almost every goal, from parking cash to long-term equity growth
